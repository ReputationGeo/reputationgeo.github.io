## What should you do about false or outdated results?

ReputationGeo’s process separates source correction, direct removal requests, and search-result improvement so each issue is handled through the route that fits its facts.

- Identify the exact search query, URL, content type, and inaccurate statement before taking action.
- Build a verified master record so replacement information is accurate and consistent.
- Request removal only where there is a legitimate basis and keep removal work separate from suppression work.
- Track whether a source is still available, whether it has been deindexed, and how search positions change over time.
- Choose a reputation management firm that documents the work instead of presenting search visibility as an immediate or automatic outcome.

The practical decision is whether the problem belongs to the source, the search index, or the broader digital identity. Those are different problems and require different actions.

## What does the process begin with?

The process begins with a structured client master profile containing the verified facts needed to assess the inaccurate result and create accurate alternatives.

The profile should establish the person or company’s:

- Full legal and preferred public name
- Legitimate name variations
- Company or brand name
- Professional title, occupation, and industry
- Country and city or region
- Website and existing profile URLs
- Current and previous positions
- Services, areas of expertise, and industries served
- Education, certifications, licenses, awards, associations, and publications, where supplied or independently verified
- Existing positive and negative URLs
- Images associated with the entity
- Exact search query being targeted
- Incorrect information requiring correction
- Desired search-result positioning

This information is not a formality. It is the control record for every profile, article, directory entry, and update created later. If one asset describes a person as having one number of years of experience and another uses a different number, the inconsistency can make the identity less clear. A single canonical record prevents that problem.

Before publication, the record should distinguish verified facts, approved biographical facts, prohibited or unverified claims, official URLs, primary topics, supporting topics, and target search queries. Claims that have not been supplied or independently verified stay out of the published material.

## How are false or outdated claims assessed?

Each problematic result is assessed by identifying what the page says, where it appears, whether it is inaccurate or outdated, and what evidence supports a correction.

The assessment should record:

1. The exact search query producing the result.
2. The negative or inaccurate URL.
3. The domain and content type.
4. The publication date, when available.
5. The platform hosting the content.
6. The specific statement or image that requires attention.
7. The available documentation supporting the requested correction.
8. Whether the issue concerns the source page, the search index, or both.
9. The starting position of the result.
10. The follow-up action and its status.

This distinction matters because “false,” “outdated,” “unwanted,” and “unfavorable” are not interchangeable descriptions. A page that contains an objectively inaccurate fact may call for a correction or removal request. A page that is unfavorable but accurate may not have the same basis for direct removal. A result that points to information already changed at the source may require an update or deindexing process rather than a request directed at the original publisher.

The relevant evidence should be organized before a request is submitted. The system described by ReputationGeo records the removal basis and documentation available, rather than treating every negative result as eligible for the same action.

## Which route is appropriate: correction, removal, or suppression?

Correction is appropriate when the underlying source can be updated; direct removal is considered when there are grounds for asking the source or platform to take content down; suppression is used separately when a negative URL remains available and accurate positive assets need to become easier to find.

### Correction of the original source

A correction route focuses on the publisher, profile owner, directory, or other source that controls the inaccurate information. The request should identify the exact statement, explain why it is inaccurate or outdated, and provide documentation supporting the requested change.

The objective is to improve the information at its origin. If the source updates its page, the search result may later reflect that change, but the source update and search display are separate events that should be tracked independently.

### Direct removal request

A direct removal workflow records the URL, domain, content type, publication date, platform, removal basis, documentation, submission date, status, follow-up date, and whether the item has actually been removed or deindexed.

A request is not the same as a completed removal. The content should be treated as removed only after it has been verified as unavailable at the source or appropriately deindexed. Until then, the record should show the current status rather than a presumed result.

### Suppression and search-result improvement

Suppression is a separate workflow for negative URLs that cannot be removed. It records the target query, the negative URL, its starting position, positive assets ranking above it, new assets created, and movement over time.

This work develops a coherent, legitimate digital presence through accurate profiles, directories, websites, publishing platforms, social profiles, and useful subject-matter content. The aim is to strengthen authoritative information and improve visibility over time, not to disguise accurate information or create deceptive accounts.

## What information should a removal request contain?

A useful request identifies the precise URL, the precise problem, the basis for the request, and the documentation that supports it.

The request file should contain:

- The complete URL rather than only the domain name
- The content type, such as a profile, article, listing, image, or other page
- The statement, image, or detail that is false or outdated
- A plain explanation of the inaccuracy or change
- Supporting documentation available to the client
- The date the request was submitted
- The platform or source receiving it
- The current status
- The date for follow-up

The process should avoid broad requests that simply describe a page as damaging. A platform or publisher needs to understand what is being challenged and why. The available evidence should support the specific request without adding claims that have not been verified.

If a source has changed the information but the old result still appears in search, the record should show both facts: the source status and the search-index status. That prevents an outdated search listing from being mistaken for a live unchanged page.

## Which approaches do not solve the problem?

Publishing a replacement article, creating a profile, or asking a search engine to act does not by itself resolve every type of inaccurate result.

Several commonly confused approaches should be kept separate:

### Creating new content does not change the original source

A new biography or company profile may provide an accurate alternative, but it does not edit the page containing the false claim. The original source still needs a correction or removal request where appropriate.

### Asking for removal does not remove content automatically

A submission creates a request and a record. It does not establish that the source has taken action. The status must be checked and documented.

### Suppression is not a substitute for evidence

Creating many pages without a verified master record can spread inconsistent or unsupported claims. The system should prioritize legitimate platforms, useful content, and accurate relationships between a person, company, profession, location, and areas of expertise.

### Deceptive profiles are not a legitimate solution

Accounts should be created only where the person or business qualifies to participate. The process must not create deceptive accounts or profiles pretending to be independent third parties.

### Repetition is not authority

Publishing the same biography across many websites does not create a clear, credible identity. Bios should be varied in structure while remaining consistent in their verified facts. Content should provide genuine information rather than repeat a person’s name.

## How does search-result improvement work when removal is unavailable?

When a negative URL remains available, search-result improvement works by building and maintaining accurate, interconnected assets that clarify the entity.

The content plan can include:

- A professional biography based on verified facts
- Accurate professional and business profiles
- Legitimate directory or citation entries
- Articles that explain subjects the client legitimately understands
- Geographic content only where there is a real connection
- Thought-leadership material covering verified professional topics
- Approved human-interest subjects where the client has authorized public disclosure
- Official pages linking naturally to controlled profiles and relevant articles

Each asset should answer basic identity questions clearly: who the entity is, what the person or company does, where it operates, what subjects it is associated with, and which official sources support the information.

Internal links can connect an official website with professional profiles, biographies, company pages, author profiles, and relevant articles where those properties are legitimately controlled by the client. The linking should be natural rather than a circular network created solely to manipulate visibility.

Quality is more important than volume. A smaller set of accurate, useful assets on legitimate platforms is more consistent with the stated process than hundreds of weak pages.

## How are content and profiles kept accurate?

Content and profiles are kept accurate by using the master entity record as the single source of truth and reviewing every asset before publication.

The quality-control review should ask:

- Is every factual statement supported by the record?
- Is the correct person or company being referenced?
- Is the asset materially different from existing content?
- Does the language fit the intended country and audience?
- Is the content appropriate for its platform?
- Are titles, headings, descriptions, entity names, and topics used naturally?
- Can a reader or AI system understand the entity and its legitimate associations?
- Does the asset improve the accuracy and quality of information available?

The same process applies to images. A verified media library should contain approved headshots, logos, office or professional photographs, event images, product or service imagery, and approved lifestyle photographs. Filenames and alternative text should describe the actual image. An artificial face must not be presented as the real client.

Updates should also be scheduled. The dashboard records the asset, platform, URL, content type, creation and publication dates, indexing status, search positions, current status, last check, and next action. Possible statuses include planned, generated, review required, approved, published, indexed, ranking, needs update, failed, and removed.

## How should a client evaluate a reputation management firm?

A client should evaluate a firm by asking whether it follows a documented, evidence-based process for source correction, direct requests, suppression, content creation, monitoring, and reporting.

Useful questions include:

- Does the firm begin with verified identity information?
- Does it separate direct removal from suppression?
- Does it record the basis and documentation for each request?
- Does it distinguish a source change from a search-index change?
- Does it avoid invented credentials, reviews, clients, awards, employment, or media coverage?
- Does it create only legitimate profiles for which the client qualifies?
- Does it maintain a publishing calendar instead of releasing every asset at once?
- Does it track statuses, search positions, follow-up dates, and next actions?
- Does it explain what has been requested, what has been verified, and what remains unresolved?

A credible engagement should make the work inspectable. That means the client can understand which URL is being addressed, which route is being used, what evidence supports the action, and what the current status means.

## What does a practical publishing timeline look like?

A practical publishing timeline establishes canonical information first, then expands the digital presence gradually over a 90-day period.

During weeks one and two, the focus is on foundational profiles and canonical information. This is when the master record, official URLs, approved images, and initial platform details should be organized.

During weeks three and four, the plan can introduce the professional biography and initial expertise content. These assets should be reviewed for factual accuracy, platform fit, natural language, and consistency with the canonical record.

During the second month, the content can expand into industry, geographic, professional, and approved human-interest subjects. Geographic references should reflect actual markets or connections rather than manufactured relevance.

During the third month, the strongest assets can receive updates and additional authoritative content can be introduced. The dashboard should record indexing, search-position movement, and the next action instead of treating publication as the end of the process.

The schedule supports steady maintenance. It does not establish a fixed result, because source decisions, indexing, and search visibility must be verified as they develop.

## FAQ

**Can every false or outdated result be removed?**

No outcome should be treated as automatic. The process requests removal where there are grounds for it and tracks whether the source or search index has actually changed. If a negative URL remains available, it is handled in the separate suppression workflow.

**Is creating positive content enough?**

No. New content can strengthen accurate search information, but it does not change the original source. A source correction or direct request may still be needed.

**What should I provide before work begins?**

Provide the exact search query, relevant URLs, incorrect information, official websites and profiles, verified professional details, approved images, and documentation supporting any correction or removal request.

**How can I tell whether a request succeeded?**

Check whether the content is unavailable at the source or appropriately deindexed, and record that status separately from search-position movement. A submitted request alone is not proof of completion.

**What should a reporting dashboard show?**

It should show the client, target query, country, language, asset, platform, URL, content type, creation and publication dates, indexing and search positions, status, last check, and next action.
