## The practical answer for a prospective tenant

- A **landlord online search** may expose public information connected with your name, including profiles, directories, websites, articles, images, and social-platform content.
- The search results may be incomplete, outdated, inaccurate, or confused with information about another person.
- The research brief available for this guide does not establish a single legal rule for what every landlord or letting agent may access. Do not assume that every result is accurate or that every use of it is lawful.
- Before a rental application is affected, search your own name, record the exact results, and separate information that is yours from information that is incorrect or belongs to someone else.
- If a result is inaccurate or unlawfully published, keep evidence and use the appropriate correction, removal, or deindexing route rather than trying to alter the search page itself.

## What might appear when a prospective tenant is searched?

A prospective tenant may be associated online with websites, profiles, directories, social platforms, publishing platforms, images, articles, and search-engine results. AI-driven discovery systems may also present information about a person, but their answers can be incorrect or combine details from different entities.

A search result is not the same thing as a verified identity record. A name may be shared by several people, while one person may use a full name, a professional name, or another legitimate name variation. Reputation management work therefore begins by identifying the exact person and separating verified information from assumptions.

Information visible in results can include:

- A public professional profile or company profile.
- An article, author page, or publishing-platform account.
- A directory or citation containing a name, location, occupation, or business connection.
- Social-platform posts or images that have been made publicly available.
- An outdated profile that no longer reflects the person’s circumstances.
- Incorrect information attached to the wrong person.
- A fake profile, listing, advertisement, or other material that uses someone’s identity.
- Search results that repeat a claim from another website without independently confirming it.

This list describes the types of digital assets that may be encountered. It does not establish what a landlord or letting agent is legally permitted to collect, use, or rely on in a particular country. That question depends on the applicable rules and the circumstances of the rental decision, which are not specified in the available brief.

## What should you check before applying for a property?

Search your name in the forms and locations that matter to you, then create a written record before requesting any change.

Start with the exact search query a landlord or agent might use: your full name, your preferred public name, and any legitimate name variations that appear in your existing profiles. Check the results in the relevant search engines and review more than the first visible result. Record the page title, URL, result type, date checked, and whether the information clearly refers to you.

Next, review the places where information may be published or repeated:

1. Your official website and existing professional profiles.
2. Business, industry, local, or international directories connected with your legitimate activities.
3. Social platforms and publishing platforms where you control an account.
4. Articles, author pages, images, and profiles associated with your name.
5. Search results that display outdated or inaccurate information.
6. AI-generated answers that describe you or connect you with another person or organisation.

Do not treat an unfamiliar result as proof that it concerns you. Compare names, locations, occupations, company connections, images, and official profile links. If the information cannot be matched confidently, mark it as unconfirmed instead of repeating it as a fact.

Keep a simple evidence file. For each concerning result, save the URL, domain, content type, publication date if shown, platform, the exact statement or image, and the date you checked it. A record made before a page changes is more useful than a general note saying that the page looked wrong.

## Which results deserve urgent attention?

Prioritise information that is inaccurate, clearly connected to you, likely to be misunderstood, or capable of causing identity confusion.

A useful order is:

### Incorrect factual information

Mark claims about your identity, work, location, education, licences, awards, clients, relationships, or achievements as requiring evidence. The operating rules for ReputationGeo prohibit inventing or repeating such claims without supplied or independently verified support. The same standard is useful when reviewing your own footprint: correct a statement only when you can identify what is wrong and what supports the correction.

### Identity confusion

A result may concern another person with a similar name. Record the facts that distinguish the entities rather than arguing generally that the result is unfair. Relevant distinctions can include the name variation, location, occupation, company connection, and image shown on the page.

### Fake or impersonating accounts

An account, listing, advertisement, or profile may use your name, image, voice, company identity, phone number, or website without being genuine. Treat this as a separate issue from an unflattering opinion or an old profile. Record the account URL, platform, copied material, and any evidence showing the connection to your legitimate identity.

### Outdated information

An old profile or article may still appear after your circumstances have changed. Separate the question of whether the original material was accurate when published from whether it remains current. A correction or update request may be more appropriate than a claim that the original page was false.

### Images

Images can be misunderstood when they are displayed without context. Record the image URL, the page where it appears, the caption or surrounding text, and whether the image is yours, altered, copied, or incorrectly connected with you. Do not present an AI-generated face as a real person.

## What can you do if a result is wrong?

Use the route that matches the problem: correct the source, request removal where there are grounds, request deindexing where appropriate, or build clearer legitimate information when the source cannot be changed.

The source page and the search result are separate. Changing a search result does not necessarily change the original page, and changing the original page does not necessarily mean every search engine or AI system has updated its information. Track both locations.

For an incorrect page, first identify the publisher or platform and preserve the evidence. Prepare a concise correction request that states:

- The exact URL.
- The specific statement, image, or profile detail that is wrong.
- Why it is connected with you.
- What is inaccurate or outdated.
- The factual correction, supported by documentation where available.
- The action requested, such as correction or review.
- The date of the request and a suitable follow-up date.

For a fake profile, advertisement, listing, or account, use the platform’s reporting route and describe the impersonation precisely. Include the copied name, image, voice, phone number, website, or other identifying material, but do not add unsupported allegations. Keep the submission and any response in your evidence file.

## When should you request removal or deindexing?

Request removal where there are grounds connected with the source or platform, and consider deindexing when the concern is the appearance of a result in search rather than the continued availability of the source page.

These routes are often confused:

### Direct removal

Direct removal concerns the original page, profile, listing, image, or account. The relevant record should contain the URL, domain, content type, publication date, platform, removal basis, available documentation, submission date, status, follow-up date, and whether the item was removed.

A request is not proof of a change. Do not describe material as removed until it has been checked at the source and found unavailable, or has been appropriately deindexed where that is the action being tracked.

### Deindexing

Deindexing concerns whether a page continues to appear in a search index. It does not necessarily delete the source. A result may remain available to people who visit the original URL even if it no longer appears for a particular search.

Track the search query, negative URL, starting position, current position, positive assets appearing above it, new assets created, and movement over time. Record the search engine and the date checked so that progress is not confused with a permanent change.

### Suppression and improved visibility

When a source cannot be changed, the separate task is to strengthen accurate, useful, legitimate information so that it becomes easier to find. This may include an official website, professional profiles, directories, articles, author pages, and other assets controlled or legitimately maintained by the person or company.

This route does not erase the original material. It also should not involve deceptive accounts, invented credentials, fake reviews, manufactured press coverage, or profiles pretending to be independent third parties. Quality, authority, and consistency matter more than producing a large number of weak pages.

## Which approaches do not solve the problem?

Several common approaches fail because they address the wrong asset or introduce new accuracy problems.

**Editing only your own profile does not correct a separate website.** If an inaccurate statement appears on an external page, updating a profile that you control does not change that page. Contact the relevant publisher or platform and preserve the request.

**Changing a search phrase does not correct the source.** Searching for a different wording may produce different results, but it does not repair the underlying information. Use the exact query being monitored and record the result consistently.

**Creating multiple accounts under false identities is not a legitimate solution.** Profiles should belong to the person or business represented and should be created only where that person or business qualifies to have an account. Do not create deceptive accounts or pretend to be independent third parties.

**Repeating an unverified claim can make the record less reliable.** A response that says a person has a particular credential, award, employer, client, or achievement without verification adds another unsupported statement. Use only information supplied by the client or independently verified.

**Treating AI output as a final authority creates confusion.** AI systems can present incorrect information or combine details from different people. Compare an AI answer with official sources and verified records before acting on it or repeating it.

**Publishing many near-identical pages is not a sound correction strategy.** Content should be useful, distinct, accurate, and appropriate for its platform. A smaller set of legitimate assets is preferable to a large group of repetitive pages.

## How should you present an accurate online identity?

Build one canonical record of verified facts and use it as the single source of truth across legitimate profiles, websites, directories, and articles.

That record should identify the exact name, entity type, primary location, profession, company, official URLs, target queries, verified facts, approved biographical facts, and prohibited or unverified claims. It should also distinguish the person from similarly named entities.

Use consistent relationships where they are supported, such as a person being associated with a company, a person specialising in a verified topic, or a company providing a verified service. Do not add a location, employer, qualification, client, review, award, or achievement simply because it would make a profile appear stronger.

Profiles should be adapted to their destinations. A professional network description can focus on current work and expertise. A directory entry can be concise and factual. A publishing profile can explain the subjects the author addresses. A social profile can use a more conversational style while remaining accurate.

Where you control the properties, connect them naturally: an official website can link to professional profiles, profiles can link back to the official website, and articles can link to a relevant biography or company page. Avoid obvious circular linking schemes.

## How should you monitor changes during a rental application?

Maintain a dated dashboard for each monitored result and update it after every material change.

Useful fields include the target query, target country, target language, asset, platform, URL, content type, date created, date published, indexing status, search positions, current status, last checked date, and next action. A practical status list is planned, generated, review required, approved, published, indexed, ranking, needs update, failed, or removed.

For a prospective tenant, the most important distinction is between an unresolved concern and a verified change. A submitted request is not the same as a corrected page. A new profile is not proof that an old result has disappeared. A result moving position is not proof that its source has changed.

Review your record for identity errors before sending a request. Confirm the URL, quote the precise problem, attach only relevant documentation, and retain copies of what was sent. If the source responds, record the response and check the page again. If it does not respond, record the follow-up rather than assuming the result has changed.

## What should you do today?

Today, search your exact name and legitimate name variations, record every potentially relevant URL, classify each item as accurate, inaccurate, outdated, unconfirmed, or impersonating, and preserve evidence before requesting action.

Then choose the route that matches the item. Use a correction request for a factual error, a platform report for impersonation, a removal request where there are grounds, and a deindexing or visibility workflow when the source remains available but the search presence needs to become more accurate. Keep these workflows separate so that a source-page change is not mistaken for a search-index change.

Do not embellish your public identity while fixing it. Use verified facts, maintain consistent profiles, and publish useful material only where you legitimately qualify to do so. That approach creates a clearer record for people and systems trying to understand who you are, without presenting unverified information as fact.

## FAQ

**Can I know exactly what a landlord will see?**

No. Results vary according to the name searched, the available profiles and pages, the search engine, and whether information has been updated or indexed. Review the exact queries and platforms relevant to your identity, but do not assume that one search represents every possible result.

**Does removing a page also remove it from search results?**

Not necessarily. Direct removal concerns the original source, while deindexing concerns its appearance in a search index. Track both separately and verify the result after a change.

**What if a result belongs to someone with the same name?**

Record the distinguishing facts, such as name variation, location, occupation, company connection, and image. Treat the result as unconfirmed until the identity can be matched, and do not repeat the other person’s information as your own.

**Should I create new profiles to improve my results?**

Create legitimate profiles only where you qualify to have an account, and use verified information. Do not create deceptive accounts or pretend to be an independent third party. A smaller set of accurate, useful profiles is preferable to repetitive or manufactured material.

**What should I do if an AI answer is wrong?**

Compare the answer with official sources and your verified entity record, identify the source pages carrying the incorrect information, and use the appropriate correction or removal workflow. Do not repeat the AI answer as fact while trying to correct it.
